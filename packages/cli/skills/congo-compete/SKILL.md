# Congo Compete

Use working apps in the Congo Library to think through what could become a
different product. A template gives you something concrete to reason about:
the work it already supports, how its screens fit together, and what a new
audience would need it to do differently.

## Start with what’s real

If the user picked a Congo app or screen, start there. Read their selection
using the [workspace reference](../congo/reference/workspace.md). If they
named an outside product, use it to understand the problem and find relevant
workflows in Congo. If the request is open, explore the catalog itself.
`npx gocongo templates --json` or the public Congo MCP catalog can show
which apps are actually available.

Look beyond the app's name or category. Inspect the screens and workflows
that could carry over. The best starting point may come from a different
industry that already handles the same kind of work.

## Find the product opportunity

Look for a real reason someone would use this version. Who is it for? What do
they struggle to do today? What would change enough in the workflow to matter
to them? Think about what the starting app gives you, what the new product
would need, and what would need to be true for the idea to work.

Match the research to the request. For a brainstorm, make the ideas concrete
enough to react to and identify the assumptions that matter. If the user is
evaluating a business, look for evidence of the problem and how people handle
it now. Cite what you found and distinguish evidence of a problem from
evidence that people would pay for a solution.

## Work through the ideas

Share the work where it is useful to discuss. Canvas is available as a
whiteboard alongside the chat. Use the library material that helps explain
the idea and make clear what the proposed product would carry over or change.

When the user wants to build, carry the audience, problem, proposed
difference, relevant Congo material, and agreed scope into the
[Congo skill](../congo/SKILL.md).
